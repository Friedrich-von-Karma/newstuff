A well-prepared enclosure is half the battle. Here is a step-by-step guide – before buying, also check the [care sheet](/en/species/) of your chosen species, as the parameters differ.

## 1. Enclosure and ventilation

A plastic box with a lid is enough. Cut holes in the lid or the top of the walls and cover them with fine mesh (metal or curtain fabric) – first-instar nymphs are very small. Ventilation matters more than it seems: without it the box gets stuffy and wet, which opens the door to mould.

For species that climb smooth walls (e.g. *Oxyhaloa*, *Aeluropoda*) smear a thin layer of petroleum jelly on the top 5 cm of the walls and keep the lid tight.

## 2. Substrate

- **Burrowing species** (*Eublaberus*, *Archimandrita*, *Macropanesthia*) – several to over ten centimetres of coco fibre or fertiliser-free soil, mixed with dry hardwood leaves.
- **Non-burrowing species** – a thin layer of substrate or just a bare floor with egg crates.
- **Desert species** (*Polyphaga*) – mostly dry substrate with one damp corner.

Collect oak, beech or hornbeam leaves dry, away from roads and fields, and ideally freeze or bake them before use.

## 3. Hides

Pieces of bark or cork and egg crates standing upright. Vertical hides matter for species that hang during the final moult – without them the wings may not expand properly.

## 4. Temperature

Most tropical species grow and breed best at 24–30 °C. The easiest way to heat is a heat mat under a third to a half of the floor – this creates a gradient and the animals choose their spot. The mat should have a thermostat. The exception is heat-sensitive species such as *Macropanesthia rhinoceros* (best at 21–25 °C).

## 5. Humidity

Instead of misting the whole box, keep a **damp zone**: part of the substrate slightly moist, the rest dry. Water the damp corner once or twice a week. Droplets on the walls all the time mean it is too wet or the ventilation is too weak.

## 6. Feeding

- **Dry food always available** – oats, fish or dog food, special insect diets. This is the main source of protein.
- **Fresh vegetables and fruit every 2–3 days** – carrot, squash, apple, courgette. Serve on a dish and remove leftovers before they go mouldy.
- **Dry leaves** – for many forest species both food and substrate.
- A water dish is not needed – nymphs can drown in it. Vegetables and damp substrate provide the water.

## 7. Cleaning

Remove food leftovers and dead animals. Replace the substrate partly when it becomes compacted and full of droppings – usually every few months. Sieve it when changing so you do not throw away small nymphs and oothecae.

## Done

An enclosure prepared like this will serve for the first months. If mites or mould appear, read [Mites, mould and flies](/en/guides/roztocza-i-plesn/).
