Feasibility study · as of 18 October 2026

Pre-owned collectible figures from China for the EU market

A profitability analysis of importing second-hand anime figures (in the best possible condition) from China to Poland and selling them across the European Union. The company: a Polish limited company (sp. z o.o.) buying stock from a related sourcing company in China. Every term is explained, so no prior knowledge of the figure market is needed.

Verdict at a glance

CONDITIONAL
YESPILOT 2027

The business makes sense as a specialist, curated shop for pre-owned mid- and high-end figures that are verified as authentic and graded on a condition scale. It does not make sense as a cheap bulk trade in the lowest-priced figures.

One number decides the outcome: the purchase price in China. At prices from typical Xianyu listings the margin is 10–14% and the business does not work. At prices from whole-collection buyouts and bargains (about 30–35% of the net retail price) the margin rises to 22–31% and the business works.

Recommendation: start in 2027 with a paid pilot (2–3 rail shipments, sales in Poland and Germany) and move to full scale only once the thresholds in “Go / no-go criteria” are met.

Three things that can kill the business and must be resolved before the first purchase:

  1. Toy law: cheap prize figures without a CE mark may be classed as toys, which cannot be imported and sold without documentation. This shapes the product mix.
  2. Full VAT: goods imported from China cannot be sold under the VAT margin scheme. We pay VAT on the full price, while private sellers on Vinted or OLX pay none.
  3. Bootlegs (counterfeits): the Chinese second-hand market is flooded with them. One counterfeit sold can destroy the brand and carries criminal liability.

How the business works

It is arbitrage (buying where it is cheaper and selling where it is more expensive) combined with a service. China has a huge number of collectors selling off their collections, so pre-owned figures are relatively cheap there. EU buyers have no easy access to them: Xianyu (China's equivalent of eBay classifieds) does not ship abroad, and buying through agents means duty, VAT on delivery, carrier fees and weeks of waiting. Our added value is “verified original, honestly described condition, shipped from Poland in 1–3 days, with an invoice and a right to return”.

  1. Buying in ChinaThe sourcing company buys on Xianyu, in second-hand shops and through whole-collection buyouts.
  2. Checks in ChinaAuthenticity and condition check, photos, bootlegs rejected before shipping.
  3. TransportConsolidated rail freight (LCL) every 4–6 weeks, 1–3 m³ at a time, 12–18 days in transit.
  4. Clearance in PolandCustoms agent, duty 4.7–6.5%, import VAT settled in the VAT return (art. 33a).
  5. Warehouse in PolandSecond check, A/B/C condition grading, professional photos, listings.
  6. SalesAllegro, eBay.de/.fr, own shop, live selling (TikTok Shop, Whatnot).
  7. DeliveryInPost, DPD, Packeta. Double-boxed so the collector's box arrives untouched.

Why the B2B model (the Chinese company sells to the Polish company) beats shipping parcels from China straight to customers: since 1 July 2026 every low-value parcel from China to the EU pays €3 duty per tariff line, and from 1 November 2026 a €2 EU handling fee is added. Bulk import into an EU warehouse avoids these charges; competitors shipping directly from China do not.

Conclusion: the general trends are strong and well documented: growing anime audiences in Europe, adult collector spending and recommerce. According to Ampere Analysis, 7 of the 10 countries with the fastest-growing interest in anime worldwide are European (Finland, Germany, Italy, UK, France, Poland, Spain). Even a 0.5% share of the niche means €0.4–1m of revenue a year, more than a small company can handle in year one. Market size is not the constraint. Margin and trust are.

Warning signs: French manga sales have been falling since their 2022 peak (−9% by volume in 2024), Japan Expo attendance is flat (about 222k), and eBay describes EU consumer confidence as weak. The blind-box craze (Labubu) cooled abruptly: resale prices in China fell by 88%. That is a warning against holding large stocks of hype-driven items.

VAT note: until total B2C sales to other EU countries exceed €10,000 a year, Polish VAT of 23% applies everywhere. Above that threshold the customer's country rate applies, declared quarterly in Poland through the OSS scheme. Opting into OSS from the start pays off, because German and French VAT is lower than Polish VAT.

Recommended order: start in Poland and Germany (months 1–3), then France and Italy (from months 4–6), and the rest of the EU through the own shop and eBay.

Competition and saturation

Japanese giants

Mandarake, Suruga-ya, AmiAmi (pre-owned), Solaris Japan, Nin-Nin-Game. Huge range and a strong reputation for condition grading. Weakness: 1–8 weeks delivery, and the EU buyer pays 19–27% VAT, the €3 duty and a €2–8 carrier fee on top. A €40 pre-owned figure costs an EU buyer €65–90 delivered.

Proxy agents

Buyee, ZenMarket (Japan), Superbuy, CSSBuy, Hoobuy (China and Xianyu). Used mainly by experienced buyers. The “rep” (replica) community that uses Chinese proxies makes this channel full of bootlegs.

EU shops

Almost all sell new figures only. Specialist pre-owned figure shops are rare (e.g. Manayga in Berlin, mostly manga). None was found in Poland.

Private sellers

Vinted, OLX, Kleinanzeigen, Leboncoin, eBay. They dominate the cheap end and pay no VAT. A race to the lowest price on cheap prize figures.

Vinted is out as a channel. Vinted Pro (business accounts) is not available in Poland, and since early 2026 Vinted has been mass-blocking Polish accounts that sell commercially. In addition, from 30 sales or €2,000 a year the platform reports the seller to the tax authority (DAC7).

Saturation verdict: the market is crowded at the bottom (cheap figures, private sellers) and under-supplied in the middle and at the top (curated, graded, guaranteed). A “verified original, graded condition, shipped from Poland” model stands out. A “cheapest price” model does not.

The golden buying rule: the purchase price must not exceed about 35% of the net retail price for scale and premium figures, and about 25% for prize figures. For a figure selling at €120 that means buying at no more than about CNY 250 (€33). Every purchase should go through a simple calculator before payment.

Other sensitivities: air freight instead of rail cuts the margin by about €2.5 (prize), €8 (scale) and €40 (premium), so premium figures lose money by air. A 5% rise of the yuan against the euro cuts the margin by 2–3 percentage points.

Logistics

Figure boxes are big and light. Air carriers then charge by volumetric weight (volume / 6000) instead of actual weight. A 20×15×30 cm box “weighs” 1.5 kg for them even if it actually weighs 0.6 kg. That is why rail is the default mode and air is only for expensive, fast-selling pieces.

  • Rhythm: a 1–3 m³ shipment every 4–6 weeks (about 100–110 medium boxes fit in 1 m³).
  • Clearance: customs agent PLN 150–400 per declaration. At 200 units per shipment that is about €1.5–2.5 per unit including local delivery.
  • Insurance: 0.25–0.35% of value. Standard policies often exclude box damage, which is critical for collectors, so negotiate it.
  • Chinese New Year (February) halts logistics for 6–8 weeks. Build stock before it.
  • Delivery to customers: InPost in Poland PLN 11–16 net, Germany €6–11, France and Italy €7–14, Spain €8–16. InPost International does not serve Germany, so use DPD, GLS or Packeta there.
  • Packaging: double box with filler: €0.6–1 (small), €1.5–2.5 (medium), €4–8 (premium). Do not cut corners here.
  • Own warehouse, not a 3PL: every pre-owned figure is a unique item that needs photos and a condition description, which outsourced warehouses handle badly. Budget €1–3 per unit for photos, grading and listing.

Polish limited company: practical points

  • Formation: via the S24 online system about PLN 250, but S24 requires a PESEL number for every shareholder and board member. A shareholder without a PESEL means a notarial deed (PLN 1,000–2,500), a sworn translator and an apostille. It takes 2–6 weeks. Minimum capital PLN 5,000.
  • 9% CIT for small taxpayers (up to about PLN 8.5m revenue). Dividends to a Chinese resident: 10% under the PL–CN treaty, with a residence certificate.
  • Estonian CIT: shareholders may be foreigners (natural persons), but it requires employees and is sensitive to related-party transactions. Not in year one. Revisit once profitable.
  • Full accounting: PLN 1,000–3,000 a month (imports, OSS, several platforms, JPK, KSeF).
  • Registrations: VAT‑R (active VAT), VAT‑UE, VIU‑R (OSS), EORI (free on PUESC), BDO, CRBR (within 7 days).
  • Social security (ZUS): a board member appointed by resolution without pay pays no ZUS. With two shareholders there is no “shareholder ZUS”.

  • New figures keep getting pricier: 1/7 figures now often cost ¥28,000–33,000. High prices for new figures push buyers towards pre-owned, which is the heart of our offer.
  • Regulation works in our favour: the €3 parcel duty (to 2028), the €2 handling fee from November 2026 and the planned full removal of the €150 exemption around 2028 raise the cost of buying directly from Asia, not of an importer with an EU warehouse.
  • Realistic company revenue in 2028–2030 (base case): €0.3–1.5m a year (3,000–12,000 figures), i.e. 0.2–0.5% of the niche.

Glossary

Prize figure
A cheap figure originally made as a prize in Japanese arcade machines and lotteries (Banpresto, SEGA, Taito, FuRyu). Simple, 15–25 cm.
Scale figure
A detailed figure at scale (e.g. 1/7 = one seventh of the character's height), static, on a base. The most important collector segment.
Nendoroid / figma
Small figures with interchangeable parts (Nendoroid: big head; figma: poseable joints). Good Smile Company and Max Factory brands.
Premium statue, GK
Large 1/4 or resin figures, often limited. A GK (garage kit) is a resin kit you paint yourself.
Bootleg
A counterfeit figure, often cast from a copy of the original mould, sold as the original. Called 祖国版 or 高仿 in China.
Authenticity hologram
A sticker on the box confirming the licence (Good Smile, Aniplex, Bandai Spirits). Not every maker uses them, and they can be faked.
A/B/C condition scale
Grading of figure and box condition, modelled on Japanese second-hand shops.
Xianyu (闲鱼, Goofish)
China's largest second-hand marketplace (Alibaba), like eBay classifieds or Vinted. Ships only within mainland China.
Proxy (agent)
A company that buys in Asia on a customer's behalf and ships it to them for a fee (Buyee, ZenMarket, Superbuy).
Gacha
Mobile games with randomised character draws (Genshin Impact, NIKKE, Blue Archive). They have many figure collectors.
Kidults
Adults and teens (12+) who buy toys and collectibles for themselves.
Arbitrage
Earning from the price difference of the same good in two markets.
B2B / B2C
Business-to-business / business-to-consumer sales.
LCL
Less than Container Load: consolidated freight, you pay for your space in a container (per m³), not the whole container.
CBM, m³
Cubic metre, the billing unit for sea and rail freight. Fits about 100–110 medium figure boxes.
Volumetric weight
A “calculated” weight from parcel volume (L × W × H / 6000). The carrier charges the higher of actual and volumetric weight.
CN code / TARIC
The EU product code that sets the duty rate. TARIC is the database of all EU rates and measures.
BTI (WIT)
Binding Tariff Information: a free, binding customs decision on a product's code. Valid 3 years across the EU.
EORI
A company's identification number for EU customs. Free.
Art. 33a Polish VAT Act
A simplification that lets import VAT be settled in the VAT return (paid and deducted at once) instead of paid in cash at clearance.
VAT margin scheme
VAT charged only on the margin, not the full price. Available for used goods bought from private individuals in the EU, not for imports.
OSS
One Stop Shop: the EU system for declaring VAT on B2C sales to other EU countries in one return in Poland.
IOSS
Import One Stop Shop: the OSS equivalent for parcels up to €150 shipped from outside the EU. Not relevant to us (bulk import).
Transfer pricing
Prices in transactions between related companies. They must be at “arm's length”, i.e. what independent companies would agree.
Fapiao
China's official VAT invoice. Private Xianyu sellers don't issue them.
GPSR
EU General Product Safety Regulation (2023/988). Duties for importers and online sellers, including for used goods.
CE, EN 71
CE is the EU conformity mark, mandatory for toys among others. EN 71 is the set of toy safety standards.
REACH
EU chemicals rules, including limits on phthalates and lead in PVC.
EPR, BDO, LUCID, Citeo
Extended producer responsibility for packaging: registers and fees in Poland (BDO), Germany (LUCID) and France (Citeo).
DAC7
The obligation for online platforms to report sellers to tax authorities.
KSeF
Poland's National e-Invoicing System, mandatory for B2B invoices from 2026.
Parallel import
Importing original goods from outside the EEA without the brand owner's consent. Can formally infringe trademark rights.
Unit margin
Contribution margin: net revenue minus all variable costs of a unit (stock, freight, duty, fees, shipping, returns, marketing), before fixed costs.
Sell-through
The share of a delivery sold within a set time (e.g. 90 days).
CAC
Customer acquisition cost (mostly advertising).
3PL
An outsourced warehouse and fulfilment provider.
Recommerce
Online trade in used goods (Vinted, eBay).
Mystery box
A bundle with random contents sold at a fixed price.
Consignment
Selling someone else's goods for a commission. The supplier is paid only after the sale.
